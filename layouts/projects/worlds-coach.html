Data Acquisition

01

Start with every touch of the tournament.

StatsBomb publishes its full event data for the 2022 World Cup as open research data. We pulled every on-ball action from all 64 matches into one flat table, then joined the 360 freeze-frames — a snapshot of where every visible defender stood at the instant each action happened.

The freeze-frames are the difference between knowing what a team did and knowing what it did under pressure.

Data Processing

02

Events are noise. Possessions are sentences.

We collapsed the feed into 11,016 possessions — one row per uninterrupted spell of play — and described each with 22 attacking features (tempo, directness, width, pressure) plus 14 defensive-shape measures computed from the freeze-frames.

Outcomes never enter the inputs. Shots, goals and xG are excluded from every feature by contract — the leakage firewall went in on day one, not after review.

EDA

03

We let the tournament name its own styles.

No preassigned tactical classes. K-means clustering over the engineered features — cluster counts chosen by internal validation and bootstrap stability, names assigned afterwards from each centroid’s fingerprint. The defensive solution is near-deterministic: stability 0.9999 across reruns.

Modeling

04

Predict the possession. Price every touch.

Shots happen in 12.5% of possessions. A model that always says “no shot” is 87% accurate and completely useless — so we select and report on precision-recall, not accuracy.

Separately, a 360-VAEP pipeline prices every single action: an isotonic-calibrated XGBoost estimates the probability that a goal follows within the next three actions — for and against — from strictly pre-action features. Cross-fitted expected threat adds positional value. The target fires 0.118% of the time; low error is cheap under that imbalance, which is why we publish PR-AUC and calibration alongside it.

Validation

05

Then we tried to break it.

Folds are whole matches — no match ever straddles train and validation. Ten matches sit in a vault that is opened exactly once, for the final number. Post-action fields in the feature set don’t warn; they raise.

{{- range $i := seq 1 64 }}{{- end }}
5 match-level folds · 54 matches Vault · 10 — opened once

Selection optimism was measured, not assumed: a nested cross-validation with fold-local clustering re-ran the whole exercise without the model ever influencing its own scoreboard. Shot: 0.527 ± 0.033 nested against 0.532 pooled. Box entry: 0.833 ± 0.014 against 0.830. The gap is noise — the headline numbers hold.

Outfield uncertainty comes from a match bootstrap that reruns the active Tournament Impact calculation on resampled tournaments. The intervals describe sampling variation and do not act as a score penalty.

Deliver

06

You’re standing in the deliverable.

The Interactive coach view on this page is the product: scenario in, plan out, a seven-phase attacking sequence you can scrub like film. Behind it sit 32 annotated team dossiers — every grade printed with its confidence interval — and a single ordered pipeline of twenty-odd scripts that rebuilds everything from the raw feed, ending in an acceptance gate that fails loudly.
